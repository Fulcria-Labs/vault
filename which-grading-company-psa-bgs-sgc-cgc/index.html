PSA vs BGS vs SGC vs CGC: Which Grading Company Should You Use?

The four majors do the same job — verify and grade your card — but they win at different things. Here's how to match the company to the card instead of grading on autopilot.

Grading is a bet, and the company you choose is part of the wager. The same card in a PSA, BGS, SGC or CGC holder can sell for meaningfully different prices, take a different amount of time to come back, and cost you a different fee. There's no single "best" grader — there's a best grader for a given card and a given buyer. This guide breaks down what each is known for, then gives you a simple way to decide.

PSA — the default for modern resale

If your card is a modern sports card you plan to sell, PSA is the safe default. It has the largest graded population and the widest pool of buyers who specifically want PSA slabs, which usually translates into the best liquidity and often the highest sale prices for the same numerical grade. The trade-off: PSA's popularity means turnaround and pricing swing with demand, and at peak times both can stretch. A PSA 10 is the grade the modern market is built around.

BGS (Beckett) — subgrades and the Black Label

Beckett's signature is the four subgrades (centering, corners, edges, surface) printed on the label. For high-end cards, those subgrades let buyers see exactly why a card earned its grade — and a BGS Black Label (a perfect 10 with four 10 subgrades) is one of the most prestigious designations in the hobby, often carrying a large premium over an ordinary 10. BGS is a strong choice for modern premium cards where condition nuance drives value, and for collectors who prefer its thicker holder.

SGC — the vintage specialist

SGC built its reputation on vintage. Its clean black-matted "tuxedo" holder frames older cards beautifully, and vintage collectors broadly trust and pay up for SGC-graded pre-modern cardboard. Historically it has also been valued for faster, more consistent turnaround. If you're grading a 1950s–1980s card, SGC is very often the right call — and in vintage circles an SGC slab can rival or beat PSA on resale.

CGC Cards — the fast-rising challenger

CGC is the long-time leader in comic-book grading (the standard the comics world runs on) and has expanded aggressively into trading cards. It competes on turnaround and price, offers subgrades on some tiers, and is a natural fit for collectors who already grade comics or TCG with CGC and want everything under one roof. Its card resale premium is still catching up to PSA on modern sports, so weigh liquidity for your specific card.

How to actually decide

Skip the tribal debates and run the card through four questions:

  1. Is it vintage or modern? Vintage → SGC (or PSA) leads. Modern sports for resale → PSA is the default.
  2. Is it high-end, where a perfect card is worth chasing a perfect label? → BGS for subgrades / Black Label upside.
  3. Who is the likely buyer, and what slab do they want? Look at recent sold comps in each holder for that exact card — the market tells you which label sells best.
  4. Does the math work at all? Add up the grading fee, shipping and your cost basis, and compare against the realistic graded value. If the grade won't add clearly more than it costs, the right move is to leave the card raw.

That last point is the one most collectors get wrong: the biggest grading mistake isn't picking the "wrong" company — it's grading cards that were never going to pencil out in any holder.

Whichever you choose, log the submission

The instant a card leaves for PSA, BGS, SGC or CGC, it disappears from your shelf for weeks or months. That's exactly when a grading submission log earns its keep: submission number, date sent, service tier, per-card cost, declared value, then the date returned and grade received. Log every submission and you learn your own gem-rate — the single most useful number for deciding what (and where) to grade next time. FAQ

Which grading company gives the highest resale value?

For most modern sports cards, PSA slabs tend to sell for the most and to the largest buyer pool, thanks to brand recognition and population size. But BGS Black Label 10s and SGC-graded vintage command their own premiums — check recent sold comps for your exact card in each holder before deciding.

Is PSA or Beckett better?

Neither is universally better. PSA usually wins on modern resale liquidity; Beckett (BGS) is prized for subgrades and the Black Label, which matter most on high-end, condition-sensitive cards. Match the company to the card and to what that card's buyers actually want.

What's the cheapest way to grade cards?

The cheapest grade is the one you skip on a card that won't gain enough value. Each major offers value/bulk tiers, but pricing and turnaround change constantly — confirm current fees, then only grade cards where the expected grade-driven value gain clearly beats the total cost.

Should I use the same company for my whole collection?

Not necessarily. Many collectors send vintage to SGC, modern resale to PSA, and premium chase cards to BGS. Consistency helps display, but resale value should win per card. Whatever the mix, keep one submission log across all of them so your economics stay in one place.
